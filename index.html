HINA

A private messenger for one family, in English and Japanese. Privacy policy and support — last updated 12 November 2026.

Contact

Questions, problems, or a request about your data: cannula_storm_8n@icloud.com. You can also report a message or block someone from inside HINA; reports go to the family's owner.

The short version

  • Your messages are kept on HINA's own server, protected by your sign-in. Only the people in a chat can read it. The family member who runs HINA runs that server and could read what is stored there. Photos, videos and voice notes are stored encrypted.
  • Two things leave HINA's server, and they are what HINA is for. To translate a message, its text is sent through HINA's server to Anthropic. To write out what a voice message says, when the person who received it taps Transcribe, that one voice message is sent through HINA's server to ElevenLabs.
  • A few features ask another service directly from your phone: weather, GIFs, word lookups and link previews. Each is listed below with what it is sent.
  • No ads, no tracking, no selling of data. HINA is invite-only.
  • You can delete your account, and everything you sent, from Settings.

What HINA keeps, and where

  • Your account — the name and language you choose, and your Sign in with Apple identifier (Apple may give us a private relay email instead of your real one). Stored in HINA's database (Supabase).
  • Messages — stored in HINA's database as readable text, with who sent each one, when, and to which chat. The database only gives a chat's messages to the signed-in people in that chat. Translations, reactions, corrections and the chat's notes on how you talk are stored the same way. Until version 1.0.1 messages were end-to-end encrypted; the app converts that earlier history to the readable form the first time it opens.
  • Photos, videos and voice notes — each file is encrypted on your phone and stored encrypted in Cloudflare R2; its key is kept with the message it belongs to, in HINA's database. Photo and video files are deleted from the server once everyone has them, or after 30 days, unless someone adds them to the chat's album. Voice notes, album items and thumbnails are kept.
  • Your profile picture, settings, and where you roughly are — stored in HINA's database, readable only by you and the people in your chats. Your location is only read while HINA is open, rounded to about 10 km before it leaves your phone, and used to show your weather in your chats.
  • Notifications — sent through Apple's push service, and they carry the sender's name and the words of the message so your iPhone can show them. Apple delivers them. In Settings you can choose to hide the words, and then a notification says only "New message".
  • Error reports — when something fails, a report goes to Sentry: what failed, error codes, counts, timings, the ids of the account, chat or message involved, and your phone's model and iOS version. Message content is removed before a report leaves the phone or the server.
  • Backups — the database is backed up every night, encrypted, and stored in Cloudflare R2. A backup holds what the database holds, messages included. Backups are kept for up to 12 months.
  • On your phone only — text in a photo is read by your iPhone itself; the photo is not sent anywhere for that. If you then tap Translate text on a photo, the words your iPhone read from it are sent for translation as a message's text is (Anthropic, below).

Who receives what

Every service that receives anything from HINA, what it is sent, and when.

  • Anthropic (translation and explanations) — what: the text of the message, a few recent messages from the same chat for context, and that chat's own notes on how you talk and which words you use. When: when a message is sent or needs translating, when you ask for a word-by-word breakdown or an explanation, when notes on how you talk are brought up to date. It all goes through HINA's server, which does not store the text and records only how much was used. Anthropic's commercial terms say it may not train its models on this content.
  • ElevenLabs (voice messages written out) — what: the one voice message you tapped Transcribe on, and the language it is spoken in. Through HINA's server, without your identity. When: only when the person who received a voice message taps Transcribe on it; a voice message nobody does that to is not sent to ElevenLabs. The words come back to that person's iPhone and are kept there. If you made a voice in Settings → My voice in a version before 1.0.1, it is deleted from ElevenLabs when you delete your account.
  • Open-Meteo (weather) — what: the rounded location (about 10 km) of a person in your chat. When: while a chat is open, the phone showing it asks Open-Meteo directly, so Open-Meteo also sees that phone's internet address. Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0).
  • GIPHY (GIFs) — what: your search words go through HINA's server, without your identity. The GIF pictures themselves are loaded by your phone straight from GIPHY, which therefore sees your phone's internet address and which GIF it asked for. When: when you open the GIF picker, and when a chat shows a GIF.
  • jisho.org (dictionary) — what: the one word you tapped, never the message. When: when you tap a Japanese word to look it up. Asked directly by your phone.
  • WaniKani (only if you connect it) — what: your own WaniKani access token, to read which kanji you have learned. No message text. When: after you enter the token in Settings, and from time to time to refresh. The token stays on your phone.
  • Websites you link to (link previews) — what and when: when you send a message containing a link, your phone fetches that page once to read its title and picture address, and keeps them with the message. When the message is shown, each reader's phone loads the preview picture from that website, which therefore sees that phone's internet address and the time. The link never passes through HINA's server.
  • Apple — Sign in with Apple; the push notification service (above); and, if you delete your account, a request to revoke HINA's sign-in.
  • Supabase — runs HINA's database and server. It holds everything described above; the text sent for translation, and a voice message sent to be written out, pass through the server it runs.
  • Cloudflare — stores the encrypted photo, video and voice files, and the encrypted backups.
  • GitHub — the nightly backup is made by an automated job there, which encrypts it before it is stored.
  • Sentry — error reports without content (above).
  • Anki (only if you use it) — when you tap Add to Anki, the word and its sentence are handed to the Anki app on your own phone.

Deleting your account

Settings → Delete account. Deleting waits seven days: you ask, you can cancel during those seven days, and after them you confirm with Delete now. Then everything you sent or set is erased for everyone — messages, photos, videos, voice notes, reactions, your picture, settings and location — your own voice, if you made one, is deleted from ElevenLabs, your Apple sign-in for HINA is revoked, and you leave every chat. Two things that belong to the chat rather than to you also stay: the chat's shared word list, and a photo someone else sent that you added to the album. Encrypted backups made before the deletion are kept until they expire, at most 12 months.

Children

HINA is for one family, by invitation. Anyone under 13 should use it only with a parent's agreement.
